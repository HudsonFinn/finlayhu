# A year of confusion, anxiety and clarity

2025 started out very well, I was doing well at work, still experiencing the highs of a new relationship and was living in a nice apartment. AI hadn't become the all consuming behemoth it is now and I was generally very happy.

## Confusion

Mid January my partner broke up with me which was a blow to the high that I was riding. Work also began heating up quicker than usual as I had a project I should have been working on for a while and had put off, it was now crunch time.

I managed to get through that period by putting my head down and focusing on work, my already excessive drinking also started to increase a fairly large amount. At peak I was having in excess of 4 pints a day 4 - 5 times a week.

This was somewhat sustainable for a while but the majority of my time being spent working and drinking meant that I was putting the rest of my life on the back burner.

In May I had to move out of the apartment that I had been living in for 2 years with my two flatmates. It was a modern 3 bed apartment in a relatively nice area of Poplar, London. One of my flatmates was moving out to live with their boyfriend and the other had got a new job on the other side of London far too distant from central London where I was working at the time.

I decided that for the first time in 6 years I was going to live alone rather than moving in with new flatmates. I was tired of living with others (not that any of my flatmates have been too bad) and wanted to learn how to live alone. I thought it would be a fun adventure.

Given my current drinking habits and dedication to work I looked for a place as close to the office as I could. Most of my drinking was with work colleagues at this time and the idea of a short walking commute was very attractive with a hangover.

I found a place for less than I was paying for my room in my 3 bed shared apartment, a tiny studio on brick lane which seemed ideal if a little small and the finish not being great. It was the first place that I saw and shook on it about 2 minutes after the viewing, I was stressed about finding a place and hadn't ever done a property hunt myself. My previous flatmates had always cared more about where we lived than I did.

I moved in. It was the most stressful move I've ever had, parking on one of the busiest streets in London is not fun doing it in a moving van is almost impossible. Stress set the tone for the rest of my time living there. The building turned out to have mice, the space was too small and I the fixtures were so beaten up and old that even after cleaning it nothing ever felt truly clean. I decided to move out in October 6 months after I had moved.

Relationships and houses have created a lot of confusion in my personal life this year. It's been a tricky one.

## Anxiety

AI, AI, AI... No one can stop speaking about it. It's tiring, kinda boring and anxiety inducing. I know and deeply understand that AI is likely going to change how we work but god do we have to speak about it ALL THE TIME. AI this, AI that. The new "agent framework" which is going to revolutionise how we use it, MCP servers, "Reasoning models", token limits. God its all so fucking boring. Its a probabilistic prediction engine which has some cool emergent properties. Yes I think it's cool. Do I want to hear about it every day? No.

The rise of ChatGPT has been on mine and everyone else's radar for years, this is the first year though that it's becoming a core part of my work and is having a large impact on my life. There is a drive within my workplace to get people using it, we are being measured for how much we are using it and it will in some way feed into performance metrics.

To some extent I can understand the drive from leadership to get engineers using it, we can be a stubborn lot which tend to stick to what we know. This is a paradigm shift for software engineering and leadership want to make sure we are at the front of it. It will increase output and as a good leader you should encourage that.

Personally I hate it and I think it may make me change careers. What was once an enjoyable experience of writing code, problem solving, learning and improving over time has become a output race to build things as fast as humanly (or artificially) possible. It's almost impossible to find the time to develop understanding of codebases and what is being written when they are evolving at a pace no one can keep up with. Code is being reviewed less, with less understanding, in the hopes of appeasing the output metrics that are being monitored.

Last year I felt like I was finally developing "taste", this ability to understand what "good" code looks like. It felt great and I felt like I was making strides in my career. This year that feels like it has gone out the window. I've been asked to learn Rust which I actually really like but I'm not yet experienced enough in it to be able to accurately review any of the AI generated code so I'm either shipping slop or not shipping at all while learning which feels awful when those around you are accepting the slop shipping to look better to leadership.

Overall I hate it but I know I have to either learn to accept it or get out of my industry. Honestly, the only option may be to learn to accept it as it seems most industries are going the same way.

Possibly partially related Amazon also announced a round of layoffs. We are still unsure who will be effected but given all the information I currently know it doesn't look like it will be me. It is a weird experience be in the limbo of career uncertainty and it definitely affected how I work and the others around me at work. Moral is at an all time low and it will be a while before the moral recovers.

Lastly, financially I've been feeling some level of anxiety. I'm actually in a really great financial position, I've managed to max out my ISA and LISA every year for the past two years and have built up an emergency fund this year that should cover around 6 months of living expenses which means if I was affected by layoffs I should be fine. The anxiety comes from the idea of this "AI bubble".

I genuinely believe that this bubble does exist even though I believe that the technology is impactful and real. The accounting of OpenAI and Nvidia looks fishy to me with the circular investment between everyone in the AI space makes me nervous. I pulled all of my money out of investments in around October after making around 20% over the last year or so, with the idea of going back in once the bubble burst and hopefully making a large profit on the recovery afterwards.

I've always held all of my investments, time in the market beats timing the market every time so the saying goes. I'm now getting fomo on the idea that maybe the bubble won't burst for multiple years and I'll miss out on all of the gains that may be made in that time. By the end of the year I'll likely look at going back in with around 2/3rds of my portfolio but will balance it differently with a large amount weighted into smaller index trackers outside of the S&P and in emerging markets that don't rely so heavily on it. I will still invest around 20% of my portfolio in the S&P to take advantage of the gains as there are still companies there which are delivering huge amounts of value.

Essentially most of my anxiety this year has been caused by AI, when this will end I do not know.
## Clarity

I decided to do the next house hunt properly, allocated more of my income to rent and accepted that I would have to move further away from work to find somewhere that was comfortable. This time I viewed 4 different apartments across different areas of London and ended up finding a 1 bedroom basement flat in Stepney Green. It was spacious, was on a lovely street and the agent showing the flat was lovely (shoutout to Base Properties).

The move was great, no stress like the last time as I booked it for the morning before Brick lane got busy and the new area was quiet. The whole thing took less than an hour. It took me a while to unpack everything but once it was done everything was so much cleaner than the previous place and I felt very happy.

Just after I had moved into the new flat I went on a Holiday to Greece with some work friends. I had a lovely first few days in the Capital Athens and then we took a boat to Naxos. Naxos was an interesting time as I was definitely the odd one out in the group and I get tired if I'm around others too long, it made for some awkward exchanges and I was exhausted by the end of the trip and was definitely coming down with some type of illness.  I still had a wonderful time in Athens and Naxos was beautiful. I would love to go back to Greece with a smaller group of closer friends, definitely to do some Greek Island hopping!

At some point around this time my thoughts also changed on drinking. I realised that my drinking was more problematic than I had previously thought and that it was eating up so much of my life and decided to reduce how much I was doing it. This started in about October where I attempted to just stop drinking completely a few times, none of these times worked and I would end up getting through something like 4 days and then would be at the pub again.

By the end of November my drinking was much more under control. The brief stints of no drinking had instead turned into periods of doing other things like climbing or still going to the pub but just drinking low/no alcohol beers. I would still drink on occasionally, maybe once a week at a friends birthday party or house party but was much more controlled and would only have 4 beers instead of the 10 I would usually have. This turned out to be much more pleasant as I would wake up in the morning remembering what had happened the night before. As other heavy drinkers will know, the lack of memory the night before is one of the worst anxieties that can be experienced in life.

To go along with the increase of exercise and reduction in drinking I also began to pay more attention to the data I was getting from my Oura ring. I'd had it since around March and would look at it occasionally but never really actioned any of the results from it. I started to monitor my sleep timings more and the quality of my sleep. Both of these began to get better and I went from a sporadic bedtime of anywhere from 11pm to 4am, to a consistent 12pm to 1am. While still not perfect I'm sleeping much better and feeling much more refreshed in the mornings.

A key part of sleeping better has been reducing my screen time. I have spent the last 3+ years going to sleep while watching movies, TV series or Youtube and just passing out while watching. You end up getting hooked into a show and watching it until 4am because "just one more episode" is never the last episode. I decided Audiobookes might help with this as at least then your eyes can be closed and you're not exposing yourself to masses of blue light. I tried this for a few weeks it wasn't really helping.

In the end I just bit the bullet and decided that I was going to sleep with no stimulation. The first few nights of doing this was very strange as my mind was going crazy while trying to get to sleep. I think it was my brain begging for some sort of stimulation which I wasn't giving it. It could take up to 2 hours just to go to sleep but I forced myself to carry on and now I can usually get to sleep in around 30 minutes of pleasant relaxation. There are still some nights where it can take multiple hours but they are getting less and less common the more consistent my sleep schedule.

Another way that I have helped myself sleep better is reading books before bed. The 30 minutes to an hour of reading before bed helps to disconnect from work and other thoughts and relaxes my mind. Along with helping me to improve my shattered attention span. I've been loving reading recently and have got through multiple books in the last month, most recently "Mr Bridge", "Superfrog Saves Tokyo" and now "Prisoners of Geography". All three of them have reignited my love of reading.

Now my drinking and sleep under control and exercising more I am feeling much more in control of life. I've started thinking more about my career and my future.

I think I need a change in my career, whether that is inside or outside Amazon I'm not sure yet. I want to get out of the frontend space somewhat and move to more backend or low level programming. The work I do at the moment is very niche and I feel like the more I do on my own personal stuff (including this website) is showing me where my gaps are. Either way, I will spend some time over the next few months preparing myself for a change. I need to keep the systems I have already setup in place and begin Leetcode practice, brush up on system design and continue to experiment with AI assisted programming.
