# On - In this Economy?
---
created: 2027-01-11 12:09
---
[Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/150249560-in-this-economy)

I got the book after enjoying many of Kyla Scanlon's instagram reels which explain economic and political events. This came off the back of reading [[On - Prisoners of Geography|Prisoners of Geography]] and I thought it might be a nice compliment to learn a bit about the how the US economy worked. 

## The bad

The first three quarters of the book it spent explaining the many different parts of the economy from supply and demand to fiscal and monetary policy. There are quite a few insights here, especially understanding more about how the federal reserve works which I doubt many people have a concrete grasp of.

However, the explanations in each section often seemed to lack depth and were occasionally confusingly written. Unless I misunderstood something some sections seemed to contain overt mistakes. Specifically in the section on repo agreements, the book seems to switch between talking about repo agreements and reverse repo agreements within it's explanation without distinguishing them. 

Additionally in the GDP calculation net exports are defined as:
> This is the dollar value of the products that we buy from other countries (imports) minus the dollar value of products that other countries buy from us (exports). 

This is just wrong.

The extensive usage of metaphors for explaining the complex economic concepts seems like a good idea to make them more accessible for the average person. I generally just found that they confused things more. Especially the weird mountain metaphor when talking about the federal reserve trying to balance it's dual inflation and employment mandate, it didn't seem to add anything.

## The good

The final section of the book which talks is more about Kyla's opinions about current economic problems and her proposed solutions, specifically the last 3 chapters ("Old guy and new theories", "Problems" and "Opportunities") is like a different book. The writing here is much better, there are less metaphors, and it has some incredible insight for humanising the economy. If this was the entire book then it may be one of my favourites.

The first part of the book is not required to understand the last part. It feels like two separate books that have been sandwiched together and neither benefit from reading the other. I hope that Kyla writes more of the second book, expanding on many of the ideas she brings up as I believe they could fill a book in their own right.

## Summary

Overall, the book is _okay_. I almost gave up on it half way through but I'm glad I didn't, the last part makes the book worth a read and I'm excited to see what Kyla writes in the future as I think there is promise of something great.
