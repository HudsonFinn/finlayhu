I find Kafka's work hard to parse, even "Metamorphosis" was a hard read. The language feels slightly disconnected from that which is spoken now. This one was both hard to parse linguistically and a vague idea to interpret. I read it in a single sitting and feel lucky that I did. Multiple separated readings would likely have meant rereading large parts to understand what had already happened.

A book narrated by a dog whose perspective you have to adopt as your own. Vague commentary on science, intelligence, isolation and belonging. It felt like there were so many parallel concepts being explored through metaphor it was hard to grasp onto any concretely. 

Parts of it brought some comfort. The narrator recalls their earlier life as a pup who at a young age sees something that they don't understand which sparks a curiosity in them that drives them for the rest of their life. When young their curiosity is entertained but never shared by others. As they grow they too find other young people who have the curiosity but do not indulge them and only entertain them in the same way their own elders had. 

The fear that they could get the answers to their questions from others if they only tried but do not as they fear they actually do not want the answers to their own questions or do not want the question answered by someone else as it would take away from their own achievement is something I found I could identify with. Ego is a driving factor of many people trying to achieve and having someone else answer your question takes away from that achievement.

Due to the avoidance of finding the answer to his questions the narrator eventually drives himself into isolation, having one remaining dog that he still spends time with. This neighbour isn't looked upon favourably. He is weak and isolated, which reflects the narrators situation, which causes the narrator to feel disgust for his only friend. 

The self awareness of the narrator mirrors how many people think about themselves. Most know their own flaws and are aware of them but are either unable or unwilling to rectify them.  As the narrator talks of so frequently about the laws of dogkind, this feels like a law of humankind. We cannot fix all of our own flaws, if we could we wouldn't be truly human. 

There are some very strange passages at the end about the narrator starving himself for his own experiments and then having visions of a hunting dog. I couldn't really parse out the meanings myself. I did look up online some of the discussion around this book and others had pointed out that the book reads very differently if you understand that the dog is completely unable to perceive humans. This does allow you to reinterpret many of the scenes and did give some closure to the more mysterious parts of the book.

It was honestly a very fun read and can be read in a couple of hours or less.
